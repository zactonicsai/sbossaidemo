# Past Marketing Emails — Voice Samples

These are five of our recent marketing emails that performed well. They show the voice we want Claude to match: warm, slightly funny, never pushy. Use these as examples when generating new ones.

**Brand:** Wandercup Tea Co. — small online tea shop, mostly women 30-55, customers care about quality and connection. We never use phrases like "act now" or "limited time only" — we'd rather lose a sale than sound like a used car ad.

Free shipping on orders over $40 as always.

## Email 5 — Sent: December 1, 2024
**Subject:** I'm not going to do a Black Friday email
**Open rate: 52% / Click rate: 9.6% / Sales: $3,120**

Hi friend,

I considered doing a Black Friday email last week. I planned the subject line. I drafted the discount. I sat on it.

Then I deleted the whole thing.

Black Friday is exhausting and it makes everyone behave a little badly. I don't love how it feels to send those emails, and I don't love how it feels to receive twelve of them in a row from my favorite brands.

So instead: nothing changed in our shop on Black Friday. Same prices. Same teas. Same shipping. If you're shopping for holidays, take your time. We are doing free gift wrap this year on orders over $50, which is a thing I genuinely love offering. We use kraft paper and twine and one little fresh sprig of evergreen.

If you'd rather not order from us this season, also fine. Buying from a local tea shop where you live is also a great option. Buying nothing is also a great option.

Anyway. Send tea, send goodwill, send a real letter sometime.

Warmly,
Maya

## Notes on Voice (for Claude to internalize)

- Lower-case "h" in "Hi friend" — never "Dear customer," never "Hey there!"
- Sign off varies by mood. "Maya," "Take it slow," "Warmly," "With genuine appreciation"
- Sentences are mixed length. Lots of one-line paragraphs.
- We use specific imagery — "the kind of tea you drink in the morning when the windows are open and you're pretending to read but really just listening to birds" — instead of generic descriptions
- We sometimes admit small things ("the snow finally gave up last week"). It feels human.
- We never pressure. "If not, that's also fine."
- Discount codes get used but framed honestly.
- Calls to action are buttons in plain language: "Shop the Spring Collection →"
