# Why Schools Should Teach Money Management

Every year, millions of students graduate high school. They know how to write a thesis statement and they know what mitochondria is. But most of them have no idea how a credit card actually works, what an interest rate is, or how to do their taxes. This is a problem and schools should fix it. Public schools should require students to take a financial literacy course before they graduate.

The first reason why is because most students don't learn this stuff at home. A lot of parents don't talk to their kids about money. Sometimes its because they are uncomfortable, sometimes its because they don't know much about it themselves. According to a study I read, more than 60% of American adults can't pass a basic financial literacy test. So how are these adults supposed to teach their kids? Schools should fill in this gap and make sure every student gets the same baseline of financial knowledge no matter what their home situation is.

Another reason is that financial decisions made at a young age can effect someone for years. When I turn 18 in two years I will probably get offered credit cards, and I might have to take out student loans for college. If I don't know how compound interest works I could make decisions that put me in debt for decades. My older brother got a credit card freshman year of college and is still paying off the balance three years later because no one ever taught him how the minimum payment thing works. He told me he wishes someone had explained it to him in high school.

Some people might argue that financial literacy is something parents should teach, not schools. They might also say that the school day is already to crowded and adding another required class would mean cutting something else. These are fair points. But like I said before, lots of parents can't or won't teach this. And as for the schedule, financial literacy could be taught as part of an existing math class instead of being its own separate class. Many states have already started doing this and it seems to work fine.

In conclusion, requiring a financial literacy course is a smart idea that would help students for the rest of their lives. The basics of how money works are not optional for being a functioning adult. We learn algebra and we should also learn how to fill out a W-2 form. If we are spending 13 years preparing students for adulthood, we shouldnt skip one of the most important things adults have to do.
